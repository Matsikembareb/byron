As someone passionate about tech, I used to list programming languages like JavaScript, Python, and C++ on my CV—thinking familiarity meant proficiency. But recently, while revisiting my portfolio, I had a wake-up call:

**Knowing the syntax of a language doesn’t mean you’re skilled in it.**

I could write basic loops and functions, sure—but was I equipped to design scalable systems or optimize user experience at a professional level? That gap between recognition and reality made me rethink everything.

## So, I made a decision.

I’m officially embarking on a comprehensive journey into system development, starting with an intensive full-stack frontend learning path.

It’s not about rushing toward frameworks or stacking buzzwords—it’s about building from the ground up:

- Building real projects weekly
- Mastering HTML/CSS, JavaScript, and modern libraries like React & Vue
- Learning testing, optimization, tooling, and deployment
- Diving deep into accessibility, performance, and engineering principles

The goal? To transform my skillset from “familiar” to “fluent,” from tinkering to creating with purpose. I want every tool I list on my resume to be backed by practice, experience, and thoughtful application.

If you’re in tech, I challenge you too: audit your skills honestly. It’s humbling—but incredibly empowering.

Follow me [on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/byronmatsikembare) if you’d like to track this journey. I’ll be sharing progress, challenges, and lessons as I grow from a code enthusiast to a system-minded developer.
